Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Pasadena, TX

When a home's original galvanized supply lines have corroded from the inside -- showing up as low water pressure, rusty water, or one pinhole leak after another in different spots -- patching each leak individually stops making sense. A full repipe replaces the aging lines with copper or PEX and solves the problem at its source.

We plan the repipe to minimize wall and ceiling openings, work room by room to keep water available where possible, and patch and prep access points for paint before we're done.

If your home's layout means a full repipe in one pass isn't practical, we'll lay out a phased approach rather than force an all-or-nothing timeline.

Signs You Need This

When To Call For Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement

  • ✓Rusty or discolored water, especially right after turning a tap on
  • ✓Water pressure that's dropped gradually over the years
  • ✓Multiple pinhole leaks appearing in different spots over time
  • ✓Visible corrosion or scale on exposed galvanized pipe
  • ✓Low water pressure at multiple fixtures at once, not just one
In Pasadena, TX

A significant share of homes in Pasadena's original neighborhoods date to the mid-20th century, when galvanized steel supply lines were standard -- those lines are now well past their typical 40-to-60-year service life, and repiping is often the point where individual leak repairs stop being the economical choice.

We also see this come up frequently during resale inspections in the area's older neighborhoods, where a buyer's inspector flags visible galvanized pipe and a repipe becomes part of the closing negotiation rather than a problem the new owner discovers later.

If the issue turns out to be limited to the underground service line rather than the pipes inside the house, that's a water line repair instead of a full repipe. A leak detection visit is usually what surfaces this distinction in the first place.

Repiping a whole house is a major decision, and it comes up most in the older parts of Pasadena, the streets that grew up around Shaver Street and Pasadena High School. We look at the pipe first and only recommend a repipe when patching stops making sense.

Our Process

How We Handle It

1

Map the existing system

We trace every galvanized run and plan the new routing before cutting anything.

2

Repipe room by room

New copper or PEX goes in section by section to keep water available where possible.

3

Pressure-test, patch, and prep

Every new line is pressure-tested, then access points are patched and left paint-ready.
