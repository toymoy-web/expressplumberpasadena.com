Toilet Repair & Installation

Toilet Repair & Installation in Pasadena, TX

A running toilet, a weak flush, or a wobble at the base each point to a different worn part -- a bad fill valve, a degraded wax ring seal, or loose closet bolts -- and most of these are same-visit fixes rather than a full replacement.

When a toilet is old enough that parts are failing one after another, or it's simply an inefficient older model, full replacement with a modern low-flow unit is usually the better long-term value, and we'll lay out that comparison honestly.

If a related part -- the wax ring, the supply valve -- looks like it's near failing too, we'll point it out rather than only fix what you called about.

Signs You Need This

When To Call For Toilet Repair & Installation

  • ✓The toilet runs continuously or intermittently between flushes
  • ✓A weak or incomplete flush that needs a second try
  • ✓Rocking or wobbling at the base when you sit down
  • ✓Water pooling at the base of the toilet after flushing
  • ✓A toilet that rocks slightly and has a stained grout line at its base
In Pasadena, TX

A running toilet is a bigger deal in Pasadena than it might seem -- a single worn flapper valve can waste thousands of gallons a month, which shows up directly on the water bill long before anyone notices the sound.

In the area's older homes, original toilets are also often higher-flow models than what's sold today, so a full replacement can cut water usage noticeably on top of fixing whatever's currently broken.

While we're already working in the bathroom, it's a natural time to mention any faucet or sink issues you've been meaning to get looked at, or a shower or tub update if you're remodeling.

Whether the house is near Strawberry Park or out toward Burke Road, a toilet that keeps running is one of the cheapest leaks to fix and one of the most expensive to ignore, since the water goes straight down the drain all day.

Our Process

How We Handle It

1

Diagnose the specific issue

Fill valve, flapper, wax ring, or bolts -- we identify which part has actually failed.

2

Repair or reset

A worn part is swapped, or the bowl is pulled and reset on a new wax ring if the base seal has failed.

3

Test the flush

We run several flush cycles to confirm the fix holds before we leave.
