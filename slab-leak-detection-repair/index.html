Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Pasadena, TX

A slab leak develops in a supply or drain line running beneath your home's concrete foundation, and by the time it's visible -- a warm spot on the floor, a damp patch appearing on carpet, an unexplained sound of running water -- it's often been leaking for a while.

We use electronic listening equipment to pinpoint the leak's exact location under the slab before any concrete is opened, which keeps the repair targeted instead of guesswork.

If what looks like a slab leak turns out to be something else -- condensation, a fixture leak, groundwater intrusion -- we'll say so rather than proceed on an assumption.

Signs You Need This

When To Call For Slab Leak Detection & Repair

  • ✓A warm spot on the floor with no obvious source (often a hot water line leak)
  • ✓A water bill that's climbed sharply with no change in usage
  • ✓The sound of running water audible even with everything off
  • ✓Damp carpet or flooring with no visible source of moisture
  • ✓A hairline crack appearing in tile or flooring near the suspected leak area
In Pasadena, TX

Slab foundations are standard across nearly all of Pasadena's housing stock, and the same seasonal clay-soil movement that stresses sewer laterals here also puts pressure on the supply lines running beneath the slab -- which is exactly why slab leaks are a real, recurring call in this area rather than a rare event.

Homes in the original neighborhoods built before modern plumbing codes are also more likely to have copper supply lines running directly through the slab itself rather than in an accessible chase, which is one more reason electronic detection matters here before anyone reaches for a jackhammer.

A slab leak is a specific, distinct case from general leak detection, and once confirmed, the repair sometimes overlaps with a broader repipe if the rest of the home's supply lines are the same age.

Most Pasadena homes sit on slab foundations over clay that swells and shrinks, so slab leaks tend to show up as a warm spot on the floor or a spinning meter, from older houses near Shaver Street to newer slabs off Red Bluff Road.
