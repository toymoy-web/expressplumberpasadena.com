Water Line Repair & Replacement in Pasadena, TX

The water service line, the pipe running from the city meter or curb stop to your house, is a different repair than in-home plumbing. A leak here often shows up as a soggy patch of yard between the meter and the house, or a water bill that's climbed with no explanation inside the home.

We locate the leak, then repair or replace the affected section using trenchless methods where the yard and soil allow it, which limits how much of the lawn or driveway gets disturbed.

If testing shows more than one weak point along the line, we'll walk through repair-versus-replace honestly rather than patch one spot and leave the rest as a question mark.

Signs You Need This

When To Call For Water Line Repair & Replacement

  • ✓A soggy or unusually green area of yard between the meter and the house
  • ✓A water bill increase with every indoor fixture checked and dry
  • ✓Noticeably low water pressure throughout the entire house
  • ✓Water pooling near the meter box itself
  • ✓A visible wet spot tracking along the yard between the meter and the house
In Pasadena, TX

The same clay-soil movement that stresses sewer laterals in Pasadena also puts pressure on water service lines running under the yard, and older galvanized or polybutylene service lines from earlier construction eras are particularly prone to failing at the joints where that ground movement concentrates stress.

Because the service line sits on the property owner's side of the meter in most cases, a failure here is the homeowner's responsibility to repair, which is exactly why testing at the meter first, before any digging, matters for confirming where the leak actually sits.

This is a different job from repiping the inside of your home, though the two sometimes get confused. If clay-soil movement damaged your water line, it's worth having us check your sewer lateral too, since the same conditions affect both.

A failed water service line shows up in the yard before it shows up in the house, often as a soft spot or a meter that keeps spinning, and Pasadena's clay soil moves enough between wet and dry spells to stress buried lines.

Low Pressure and Service Lines

When Pressure Drops or the Line Fails

Pressure problems and service line problems are closely related, and they are easy to confuse. Where the symptom shows up tells us where to look.

Low pressure at every fixture

If the whole house is weak, the cause is usually upstream of the fixtures, in the main shutoff valve, a pressure regulator, or the service line itself.

Low pressure at one fixture

A single weak faucet or showerhead usually points to a clogged aerator, a partly closed stop valve, or a worn cartridge at that fixture.

Water in the yard or at the meter box

Soft ground, standing water near the meter, or a meter that spins with everything off suggests a leak in the line. We find where it starts.

No water at all

A closed or failed valve, a broken line, or a problem on the utility side can all cause it. We confirm the cause quickly so you know who needs to fix what.

The City of Pasadena states that customers are responsible for the water service lines that run from the meter to their homes, which is why a quick test at the meter is the first step on most calls.
