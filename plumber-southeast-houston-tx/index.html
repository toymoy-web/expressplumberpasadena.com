Plumbing Service in Southeast Houston, TX

Southeast Houston is the stretch of the City of Houston that runs right up against Pasadena's western edge, from the Hobby Airport area and Gulfgate down through Park Place, Meadowbrook, Edgebrook, and the Southbelt and Ellington side. Despite the Houston mailing address, many of these streets sit closer to our Pasadena dispatch point than to downtown Houston, and the 77017, 77034, and 77075 ZIP codes each share a boundary with Pasadena's 77504.

Most of the housing here went up in the postwar decades, which is why brick ranch homes from the 1950s and 1960s are so common on these blocks. We treat Southeast Houston as part of our home territory rather than a distant add-on, because the plumbing in these older neighborhoods behaves a lot like the plumbing in Pasadena's own oldest streets.

About Southeast Houston

The area is anchored by William P. Hobby Airport, which grew out of a private airfield opened in 1927, and by the Glenbrook Valley Historic District, a City of Houston historic district known for its mid-century modern architecture.

Gulfgate Center, one of Houston's first malls, and the Sims Bayou Urban Nature Center sit along the northern side, while Glenbrook Park Golf Course and Gus Wortham Park Golf Course supply much of the green space.

San Jacinto College's South Campus on Beamer Road, in the 77089 ZIP code, anchors the southern end of this side of town.

Because so much of the area is mid-century housing on slab foundations over Gulf Coast clay, the usual suspects show up on service calls: galvanized supply lines that rust from the inside, cast iron drains that have worn thin, and water heaters that have outlived a previous owner or two.

Sims Bayou runs along the northern edge, so lots near the bayou and its drainage ditches also need extra attention to sewer cleanouts and yard drainage after heavy rain. With Beltway 8 and I-45 both close, a technician can reach most of these addresses without crossing the Ship Channel.

Why It's Different Here

Southeast Houston's older streets share the same postwar building boom that shaped Pasadena, so we expect original plumbing until a house proves otherwise. A 1950s brick ranch with original drain lines under the slab is a very different job from a newer townhome near Beltway 8, and we scope each visit to the house in front of us.

Postwar homes here lean on three jobs: repiping when galvanized lines finally give out, sewer line repair for aging cast iron under the slab, and water heater replacement once a tank passes its prime. For a drain that keeps backing up near the bayou, hydro jetting is often the next step after a basic snaking, and we reach this side of town through South Houston on the same route.

Southeast Houston's 77017, 77034, 77075, and 77087 line up along Pasadena's 77504, 77502, and 77506, with 77061 and 77089 alongside them, so water heater repair and leak detection calls from any of those ZIPs ride the same dispatch route as a call inside Pasadena.
