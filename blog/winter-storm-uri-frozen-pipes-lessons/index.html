What Winter Storm Uri Taught Pasadena Homeowners About Frozen Pipes

By the Pasadena Express Plumbing team · Published January 8, 2026

February 2021's freeze cost the average Texas claim about $27,000. Pasadena doesn't need hard freezes often, but the lesson holds.

In February 2021, Winter Storm Uri brought days of below-freezing temperatures across Texas, and roughly 16% of Texans reported pipes freezing and bursting in their homes during the event, with the average resulting insurance claim landing around $27,000. Pasadena, sitting on the Gulf Coast, isn't built for that kind of cold, and neither is most of its plumbing.

The core problem isn't the cold itself; it's that Texas homes, including most in Pasadena, are built for a climate that rarely sees hard freezes, so pipes run through uninsulated exterior walls, attics, and garages that homes in colder states would never allow.

Why Pipes Actually Burst

A pipe doesn't usually split from the ice itself sitting in one spot -- it splits because ice blocks the line somewhere, and pressure builds behind that blockage with nowhere to go, until a weak point in the pipe gives way.

That's why the failure often shows up away from the actual frozen section, and why a pipe can crack during the freeze but not visibly leak until it thaws and the water starts moving again.

What Uri Actually Changed for Gulf Coast Homes

Before 2021, a lot of Houston-area homeowners treated freeze prep as something that mattered further north. Uri made it clear that a Gulf Coast home's exposed hose bibs, uninsulated attic runs, and shallow-buried service lines are genuinely vulnerable when a cold snap does arrive, even if it only happens once every few years.

The insurance industry noticed too -- claims data from that single event now shapes how several carriers evaluate freeze risk on Texas policies, which is part of why some insurers now ask about pipe insulation and exposed plumbing during underwriting.

Practical Prep for Pasadena Specifically

Disconnect and drain garden hoses before a forecasted freeze, and wrap or cover exposed exterior hose bibs -- these are the first things to fail because they're the most exposed part of most Pasadena homes' plumbing.

Let faucets drip during a hard freeze warning to keep water moving through the lines, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so household heat can reach the pipes.

  • ✓Disconnect and drain garden hoses before a freeze warning
  • ✓Insulate or cover exposed exterior hose bibs and hose connections
  • ✓Let faucets drip during hard freeze conditions to keep water moving
  • ✓Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls
  • ✓Know where your main water shutoff valve is before you need it

If a Pipe Already Burst

Shut off the main water valve first, before anything else -- that single step limits how much water damage accumulates before help arrives. Our burst pipe repair page covers what to do in the minutes right after you find one.

Once the immediate emergency is handled, it's worth having a leak detection check run on the rest of the house -- a freeze severe enough to crack one pipe often stresses others that haven't failed yet.

How This Compares to Colder-Climate Homes

In states that see freezing temperatures every winter, building codes require deeper-buried water lines and insulated interior pipe runs as standard practice. Chicago requires water lines buried at least five feet deep, for instance, specifically to stay below the frost line.

Texas cities, Pasadena included, typically require much shallower burial depths, since hard freezes are rare enough that deep burial wasn't historically considered necessary. That's a reasonable trade-off most years, but it's exactly what made Uri's damage so widespread when the rare event actually happened.

What Insurance Companies Learned

The scale of claims from Uri changed how several major carriers underwrite Texas policies, with some now asking specifically about pipe insulation, exposed plumbing, and whether a home has taken any freeze-prevention measures.

That shift is worth knowing about even outside of an actual storm: some carriers offer premium adjustments for documented freeze preparation, which is one more reason basic prep pays for itself beyond just avoiding the direct cost of a burst pipe.
