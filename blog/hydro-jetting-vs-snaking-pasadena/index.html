Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking for Pasadena Sewer Lines

By the Pasadena Express Plumbing team · Published August 22, 2025

Both clear clogs. Only one actually cleans the pipe. Here's when each is the right call.

Snaking punches through a blockage and restores flow. Hydro jetting scours the entire interior wall of the pipe clean, using a high-pressure water stream, which removes the buildup that caused the clog in the first place rather than just breaking through it.

For a single, simple clog, snaking is often the faster, less expensive fix. For a line that clogs repeatedly, or shows grease or scale buildup on a camera inspection, hydro jetting is usually the one that actually solves the underlying problem.

Neither method is inherently better in the abstract: the right choice depends entirely on what's actually happening inside your specific line, which is exactly why we don't default to one or the other without first understanding the problem.

How Each Method Actually Works

A drain snake, whether a hand-crank consumer tool or a professional cable machine, physically breaks through a blockage using a rotating cable, clearing a path for water to flow again.

A hydro jetter runs water at high pressure through a specialized nozzle that sprays both forward and backward as it's pulled through the line, cutting through buildup on all sides of the pipe rather than just punching a hole through the middle of it.

Why This Matters More in Older Pasadena Homes

A lot of Pasadena's original neighborhoods still run cast iron drain lines, which corrode and roughen on the inside over decades. That rough surface catches grease, soap scum, and debris far more easily than smooth PVC, which is exactly why the same drain can clog again within weeks of being snaked.

Hydro jetting addresses that root cause directly by clearing the buildup coating the pipe walls, not just the specific clog sitting in one spot, which is why it tends to last longer against repeat clogs in these older lines.

When Snaking Is Still the Right Call

For a single clog with no history of recurrence, snaking is usually the more practical, lower-cost first step. There's no reason to jet a line that's never had a repeat problem.

Snaking is also the appropriate choice when a camera inspection shows the pipe's condition isn't solid enough to safely handle jetting's water pressure, which does happen with severely deteriorated older lines.

When to Skip Straight to Jetting

If a line has already been snaked and clogged again within weeks, that's the clearest signal that jetting is the better next step rather than repeating the same temporary fix.

Multiple drains backing up together, or a camera inspection showing grease or scale coating the pipe interior, both point toward hydro jetting as the right call from the start rather than snaking first.

What a Combined Approach Looks Like

For some jobs, snaking first to clear an immediate blockage, then jetting afterward to address the underlying buildup, is actually the most practical sequence. It restores flow right away while still solving the longer-term problem in the same visit.

We'll recommend this combined approach when a camera inspection shows both an active blockage and significant buildup along the rest of the line, rather than treating it as strictly one method or the other.

What Happens If You Skip Straight to Jetting Unnecessarily

Jetting a line that only needed a simple snake isn't harmful to the pipe, but it does mean paying for more service than the situation required, which is exactly why we recommend a camera look first rather than defaulting to the more intensive option for every call.

The goal is matching the tool to the actual problem, not always reaching for the most thorough option regardless of what's needed.
