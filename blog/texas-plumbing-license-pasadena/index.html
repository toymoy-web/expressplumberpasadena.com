Texas Plumbing Licenses: A Pasadena Homeowner's Guide

By the Pasadena Express Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2025

Texas licenses plumbers at the state level, not city by city. Here's what that means when you're vetting who to hire.

Texas licenses plumbers through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, a single statewide authority rather than a patchwork of city-by-city licensing boards. That's different from how some states handle it: Pennsylvania, for example, licenses plumbers at the municipal level, so a system that works in one state doesn't automatically transfer to how Texas does it.

For a Pasadena homeowner, this means any plumber working on your home should carry a valid Texas state license, regardless of what city they're physically based in, and that license is checkable through the state board rather than a local city office.

This distinction matters more than it might seem for a practical reason: a plumber operating without proper licensing has no real accountability if something goes wrong, and no state board oversight standing behind the work if a dispute arises later.

Why This Matters Beyond Just Compliance

Licensing exists because plumbing work done incorrectly can create real safety hazards: improper gas line connections, backflow that contaminates drinking water, or drain work that violates code and causes problems for a whole property, not just one fixture.

A licensed plumber has demonstrated a baseline of tested knowledge and is accountable to the state board if something goes wrong, which an unlicensed handyman working under the radar simply isn't.

What to Actually Check

Ask for the license number directly and verify it's current: a legitimate business will have this on hand without hesitation, since it's public information they're required to be able to provide.

  • ✓Ask for the plumber's Texas state license number
  • ✓Confirm the license is current and in good standing
  • ✓Check that any major work pulls the required permit where applicable
  • ✓Confirm the business carries liability insurance, not just the individual technician's license

Where Licensing Requirements Get Stricter

Gas line work, backflow prevention testing, and any plumbing tied to a new construction permit all carry additional requirements beyond a base plumbing license, since the stakes of getting them wrong are higher.

If you're having gas line work done or need backflow testing and certification, confirm the technician specifically handles that category of work, since not every general plumbing license covers every specialty task at the same level.

A Practical Note on Permits

Sewer line replacement, gas line installation, and significant repiping work typically require a permit and inspection in Pasadena, and a licensed, established plumber handles that as a normal part of the job rather than something you have to chase down separately.

If a quote seems unusually low and doesn't mention a permit for work that clearly needs one, that's worth asking about directly before agreeing to anything.

What Happens If Unlicensed Work Goes Wrong

Beyond the immediate safety risk, plumbing work done without proper licensing and permits can create real problems later: at resale, an inspector may flag unpermitted work, which can delay or complicate a sale.

Insurance claims related to plumbing damage can also be more complicated if the underlying work that caused or contributed to the problem wasn't done by a licensed professional, since some policies specifically address this.

How Licensing Requirements Compare Across States

Texas runs licensing at the state level through a single board, which is different from Colorado's state-level DORA system, California's CSLB, and Pennsylvania's municipal-only approach: each state genuinely does this differently, so assumptions from one state don't transfer to another.

If you've moved to Pasadena from a state with different licensing rules, it's worth knowing this isn't a lesser or informal version of what you're used to: Texas's system is simply structured differently, with its own board, its own verification process, and its own standards.

Local Note

Pasadena Note: Checking a Plumber's License

The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners lists four license types: Tradesman Plumber-Limited, Journeyman, Master, and Plumbing Inspector. Licenses renew annually and require 6 hours of continuing education.

The Board also runs a public license search, so you can confirm a plumber's license before anyone starts work in your Pasadena home.
