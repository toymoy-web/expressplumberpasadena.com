Why Your Water Bill Spiked: Common Causes in Pasadena Homes

By the Pasadena Express Plumbing team · Published December 5, 2025

Nothing looks different in the house, but the bill jumped. Here's what usually causes it and how to actually find the source.

A water bill that jumps with no obvious change in household usage almost always means a leak somewhere in the system, and in most cases it's a leak you can't see: running toilet, a slab leak under the foundation, or an irrigation line leaking underground.

The good news is that the size of the jump usually gives a rough clue to where to look -- a modest increase often points to a running toilet, while a dramatic spike points toward something bigger, like a supply line leak that's running continuously.

It's worth adding that a spike isn't always dramatic and sudden -- sometimes it's a steady, smaller increase over two or three billing cycles that's easy to write off as normal variation until you actually compare the numbers side by side.

Start With the Water Meter Test

With every fixture in the house off, check your water meter, wait 15 minutes without using any water, then check it again. If it's moved at all, water is flowing somewhere in the system even though nothing is visibly running.

This single test rules a lot in or out before anyone starts guessing, and it costs nothing but a few minutes.

The Usual Suspects, Ranked by How Often They're the Cause

A running toilet is the single most common cause of an unexplained bill increase -- a worn flapper valve that doesn't fully seal can waste thousands of gallons a month without ever overflowing or making an obvious sound.

  • ✓A running toilet with a worn flapper or fill valve (most common cause)
  • ✓An irrigation system leaking underground, especially at old or corroded fittings
  • ✓A slab leak under the foundation, more likely in a Gulf Coast clay-soil home
  • ✓A dripping faucet left unnoticed for weeks or months
  • ✓A water softener stuck in a regeneration cycle

When It's Likely a Slab Leak

If the meter test shows water moving and you've ruled out every visible fixture, a slab leak becomes the more likely explanation, particularly in Pasadena where seasonal clay-soil movement puts real stress on supply lines running under the foundation.

A warm spot on the floor, especially over a hot water line, or the sound of running water audible even with everything off, both point toward a slab leak specifically rather than a fixture issue.

Finding It Without Tearing Up the House

Electronic leak detection equipment locates a hidden leak's position acoustically before anyone opens a wall or floor, which keeps the eventual repair targeted instead of exploratory.

If the source turns out to be the main line between the meter and the house rather than something inside, that's a water line repair rather than an in-home fix, and it's worth knowing the difference before assuming the worst.

A Simple Way to Track It Going Forward

Once a leak is resolved, keeping a rough monthly log of your water bill makes the next unusual spike obvious immediately rather than something you notice three months later after it's cost real money.

Many water utilities, including services covering the Pasadena area, also offer usage alerts that flag unusual consumption directly -- worth setting up once, since it does some of this monitoring automatically going forward.

What a Normal Bill Actually Looks Like

Average household water usage in the Houston area runs somewhere in the range of a few thousand gallons a month for a typical family, though this varies with household size, irrigation use, and season -- summer bills are reasonably higher due to outdoor watering.

The goal isn't memorizing an exact number, but noticing your own household's normal range well enough that a real departure from it stands out quickly, rather than blending into normal month-to-month variation until it's a much bigger problem.
