Gas Line Installation & Repair

Gas Line Installation & Repair in Pasadena, TX

If you smell gas anywhere in or around your home, leave the property and call the gas utility's emergency line before calling us. Once the utility confirms it's safe, we handle the diagnosis and repair of the line itself.

Beyond emergencies, we install and reroute gas lines for new appliances -- ranges, tankless water heaters, outdoor grills, generators -- with proper sizing, pressure testing, and the permit and inspection each install requires.

If the smell or symptom points to something beyond the line itself, we'll flag it honestly rather than just patch the immediate connection and move on.

Signs You Need This

When To Call For Gas Line Installation & Repair

  • ✓A rotten-egg smell near a gas appliance or meter
  • ✓A hissing sound near a gas line or connection
  • ✓A gas appliance with an unstable or yellow (not blue) flame
  • ✓Dead vegetation in a patch of yard with no other explanation
  • ✓A visible corroded or discolored fitting near a gas appliance connection
In Pasadena, TX

A number of homes in Pasadena's older sections were built with gas lines sized for the appliances of that era, which don't always meet the demand of a modern tankless water heater or outdoor kitchen without a line upgrade -- something we check before quoting a new appliance install.

With the Ship Channel's industrial and petrochemical presence nearby, we also see more commercial and light-industrial gas line calls in Pasadena than in a purely residential suburb, which keeps our crews familiar with both scales of work.

New gas line work for a tankless water heater conversion is one of the most common reasons we get called for this. Commercial properties should see our commercial plumbing page for broader coverage.

Gas line work across Pasadena, from homes off Beltway 8 to the neighborhoods near San Jacinto College's Central Campus on Spencer Highway, is permitted and pressure-tested, because a gas fitting is not the place to cut a corner.

Our Process

How We Handle It

1

Confirm safety first

For any suspected leak, the utility's safety check comes before we touch the line.

2

Size and route the line

New installs are sized to the appliance's BTU demand and routed to code.

3

Pressure-test and permit

Every line is pressure-tested and signed off through the required inspection.
